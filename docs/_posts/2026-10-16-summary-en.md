### [Neural networks are clean: a contested interpretability claim](https://already-clean.goodfire.com/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Nick Cammarata’s “Neural networks are clean” essay argues that neural networks are substantially easier to interpret than his earlier OpenAI mechanistic-interpretability work suggested, and that “sequencing” models can explain and anticipate failures. The evidence so far is a launch post shared via X, not an independent study; Hacker News commenters sharply dispute whether the claims overreach what has actually been proven.

hackernews · themgt · Oct 15, 16:04 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50105647)

**「Background」** Mechanistic interpretability research has long treated polysemanticity—single neurons that respond to multiple unrelated concepts, such as InceptionV1&\#x27;s &\#x27;cat and car&\#x27; neuron—as a fundamental obstacle, often attributed to superposition. That pessimistic view was crystallized in the 2020 Distill article &\#x27;Zoom In,&\#x27; which examined such messy neurons directly. Nick Cammarata&\#x27;s October 15, 2026 essay &\#x27;Neural Networks Are Already Clean,&\#x27; published with Goodfire, revisits those canonical examples and argues—as a working hypothesis—that the apparent messiness came from &\#x27;ordinary, mostly fixable issues&\#x27; rather than superposition, with a technical companion paper as support.

**「Impact」** For interpretability researchers, the argument challenges the usual pessimistic reading of polysemanticity and tool failures, pointing the field toward full-model “sequencing.” The supplied sources do not yet substantiate that the approach scales, so the practical effect remains uncertain.

**「Community discussion」** Commenters split over the claim: sebmellen recommends the launch post for context, while bluejay2387 calls the claims an overreach, noting that progressive feature complexity and the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis are not incompatible with parameter overloading. aesthesia cautions that the tone resembles Anthropic’s early SAE enthusiasm, and antics reads the essay as an evidentiary brief for “sequencing the AI genome.”

### [Neural networks self-assemble their own learning machinery](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1x6hmyg/how_artificial_neural_networks_build_their_own/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Researchers reported in PNAS on October 9, 2026 that randomly interconnected neuron-like units governed by shared plasticity rules can self-assemble their own learning motifs and perform pattern recognition and image classification with performance similar to backpropagation-trained networks in the tested comparisons. The work shifts from designing a learning circuit directly to specifying local rules from which learning machinery organizes itself, motivated by biological plausibility rather than as a proposed replacement for backpropagation in large-scale models. The authors caution that this self-organization does not establish that the brain uses the same mechanism, and the demonstrated tasks remain limited.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Dorian182 · Oct 15, 10:01

**「Background」** Conventional deep learning relies on backpropagation, which requires explicit wiring and global error signals to adjust network weights. This research instead explores whether simple, shared local plasticity rules can let randomly connected units build effective learning procedures without directly designing that machinery, a question motivated by how biological synapses might self-organize.

**「Impact」** For machine learning and neuroscience researchers, the result suggests that local plasticity rules can match backpropagation on small image-classification tasks without explicit credit assignment, but the practical significance depends on whether the self-assembled motifs remain stable across different initial connectivity and scale beyond the demonstrated benchmarks. Follow-up work should test those conditions before treating the approach as a viable alternative learning paradigm.
